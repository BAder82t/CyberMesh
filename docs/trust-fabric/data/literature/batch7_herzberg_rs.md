- **Claim checks:**
  - A1: Q: How are shares renewed each period; zero-constant-term random polynomial, sub-shares added? -> A: Yes. Each server P_i picks a degree-k polynomial delta_i with delta_i(0)=0, sends delta_i(j) secretly to P_j, and each server adds the received values to its old share, then erases everything but its key. | read: full text (Section 3.3, Share Renewal Protocol) | evidence: "pi picks k random numbers ... define a polynomial ... whose free coefficient is zero"; "computes its new share x_i + (u_1i + ... + u_ni) (mod q) and erases all the variables" (OCR)
  - A2: Q: Assumptions? -> A: (i) common broadcast channel C with instant delivery; (ii) synchrony/global clock; (iii) local randomness; (iv) at most k of n servers corrupted in each period, k < n/2 (the paper says "k = n/2 - 1" supported), servers corrupted during an update count for both adjacent periods; (v) adversary hears and can inject on C but cannot modify honest servers' messages or block delivery; (vi) computationally bounded adversary; secure public-key encryption and signatures, and a VSS (Feldman or Pedersen); (vii) old shares and variables are erased; (viii) system securely initialized, and a corrupted server is removable (reboot) when detected, with minimal trust in system management for installing correct programs and public keys. | read: full text (Intro, Section 2.1, 2.2) | evidence: "We assume that the adversary corrupts no more than k out of n servers in each time period, where k must be smaller than n/2"; "we assume a broadcast channel and synchrony"
  - A3: Q: Information-theoretic or computational? -> A: Computational overall. The text states the adversary is computationally bounded and the scheme relies on secure encryption, signatures and VSS (discrete-log based Feldman). Information-theoretic secrecy is proved only for the bare renewal protocol assuming perfectly secret transmission of sub-shares; with explicit encryption, secrecy reduces to the encryption. Feldman VSS is computational; Pedersen VSS gives information-theoretic secrecy of the shared value, and the authors say their solution works with either. Encryption and signatures remain computational. | read: full text (Sections 2.1, 2.2, 3.3) | evidence: "assume the adversary to be computationally bounded"; "first scheme protects the secrecy ... in a computational sense, while the second provides information theoretic secrecy"; "we assume here that the shares are transmitted ... with perfect secrecy. This allows us to prove the information-theoretic secrecy"
  - A4: Q: Detecting or recovering a corrupted share? -> A: Section 4. Each server stores exponents g^{x_j} of all current shares (updated homomorphically each period); servers broadcast signed copies, decide by majority, and identify servers whose exponent differs. A share recovery protocol (basic and full) then lets honest servers restore the share to its owner without revealing it to others. | read: full text (Section 4, 4.1) | evidence: "each server decides by majority on the current proper set of share exponents" (OCR)
  - Extra: main result, Theorem 6: with at most k corrupted servers per period, the protocol is a "secure and robust proactive secret sharing scheme" (computational model).
- **Notes:** The scheme uses degree-k polynomials with threshold k+1 (not k as in the abstract's "k out of n" phrasing). Authors' affiliation in the paper: IBM T.J. Watson. The paper says the full formal definitions are deferred to a "complete version". The phrase "honest majority" appears as k < n/2 (k+1 honest servers). Scanned copy: Springer PDF; no open authors' copy found. The related IBM patent US5625692A (Google Patents) shows the same renewal with the same wording, but was not used as the source.
